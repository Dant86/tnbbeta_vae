First, let's discuss convergence.

\begin{theorem}[Modality of SphericalTNBBeta]
\label{thm:modality}
Fix $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{S}^{d - 1}$. For any $p \in (0, 1)$ and $q \in [0, 1)$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item if $\varepsilon > (d - 1)/2$, $\mathrm{SphericalTNBBeta}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, p, q, \varepsilon)$
    is unimodal;
    \item if $\varepsilon < (d - 1)/2$, $\mathrm{SphericalTNBBeta}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, p, q, \varepsilon)$
    admits two modes at $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and $-\boldsymbol{\mu}$, and may admit a third interior mode
    depending on the concentration parameter $q$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence of SphericalTNBBeta]
\label{thm:convergence}
Fix $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{S}^{d - 1}$, $q \in [0, 1)$, and $\varepsilon > (d - 1)/2$. As
$p \to 1$, $\mathrm{SphericalTNBBeta}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, p, q, \varepsilon)$ converges to
$\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$, and mass is concentrated within $O(1 - p)$ of $\boldsymbol{\mu}$.
\end{theorem}

The vMF distribution always has a cap-shape, and trivially converges to a dirac mass about
$\boldsymbol{\mu}$ as $\kappa \to \infty$.

Moving on to the graph representation task, I ran experiments with both the TNBBeta-VAE and S-VAE on
PubMed last week, but saw nearly identical results with both posteriors. My hypothesis was that that
dataset had too few connected communities, and as such a unimodal posterior was sufficient to describe
the latent space. The Stanford DBLP dataset is significantly more diverse. It's a co-authorship network
built on research papers in computer science. Each node is an author, and they are connected if they
have published at least one paper together. Ground-truth communities are defined by publication venue.
Per Stanford's dataset statistics, the graph has a clustering coefficient of $\approx 0.63$, indicating
that there are many connected communities, indicating that a VAE with a multimodal latent could aid in
graph representation problems.

To test this hypothesis out, I trained a graph-VAE on com-DBLP, and evaluated link prediction (i.e.
predicting whether two held-out nodes are actually connected). Note that there are no node features in this
particular dataset. To assess performance, I evaluated AUC and AP both on the entire test set, as well as
buckets of the test set grouped by the number of communities the contained nodes belong to. We see that
the TNBBeta-VAE outperforms both the S-VAE and the power-spherical VAE:

It is important to note that in all cases, the SphericalTNBBeta posterior always had
$\varepsilon < (d - 1)/2$, meaning that this particular task leveraged multimodality of the
posterior. \Cref{thm:vmf-ps-unimodal} shows that this cannot possibly hold for the vMF or
power-spherical distributions:
\begin{theorem}[Unimodality of vMF and Power Spherical]
\label{thm:vmf-ps-unimodal}
For every $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{S}^{d - 1}$ and every $\kappa > 0$, the
$\mathrm{vMF}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \kappa)$ and $\mathrm{PowerSpherical}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \kappa)$
distributions are unimodal, with unique mode at $\boldsymbol{\mu}$.
\end{theorem}
